<!-- # Ways to sieve through new papers and look for related ones -->

This project lists ideas for how to get updated and hear of new papers. Feel free to use any of the tools and to share new tools that you created\found\use.

## Start here — what do you want to do?

| I want to… | Go to |
| --- | --- |
| See what's **hot right now** across the field | [What's trending](#whats-trending) |
| **Follow specific people / topics** and get a feed tuned to me | [Build a personal feed](#build-a-personal-feed) |
| Make sure I **don't miss anything** in my area | [Cover a whole field](#cover-a-whole-field) |
| **Save and organize** what I've read | [Keep track of what you've read](#keep-track-of-what-youve-read) |
| Find papers **related to one I already have** | [Find related papers](#find-related-papers) |

*Rule of thumb: use **trending/feed** tools to discover, **search/related** tools when you have a specific paper or question, and a **reference manager** so you never lose a paper twice.*

## What's trending

*Best for a quick pulse on the field — what people are reading and talking about this week.*

- **[alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/)** — trending arXiv papers with public discussion threads.
- **[Twitter/X](https://twitter.com)** — the fastest signal for new work. Follow field-specific lists rather than individuals to cut noise (example [lists](https://twitter.com/LChoshen/lists/memberships) in NLP and ML).
- **[labml](https://papers.labml.ai/)** — surfaces the papers Twitter is buzzing about, ranked.
- **[trendingPapers](https://trendingpapers.com)** — a site dedicated to what's trending.
- **[Papers with Code — Trending](https://paperswithcode.com/)** — trending papers, weighted toward those with released code.

<details>
<summary>Tips</summary>

- Trending ≠ important — it favors flashy and recent. Use it to *notice*, not to decide what matters.
- Following a couple of good curated lists beats following 200 accounts.
</details>

## Build a personal feed

*Best for staying current on your own topics — set it up once, then let recommendations come to you.*

**Follow specific researchers**
- **[Twitter/X](https://twitter.com)** — follow authors to catch work as they announce it.
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/me/recommendations)** — follow authors and get recommendations in your library feed.
- **[ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/)** — author-following (less popular in computer science).
- **[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/citations.html)** — get email alerts for a key author's new and related work ([how-to](https://scholar.googleblog.com/2017/10/follow-related-research-for-key-authors.html#:~:text=To%20follow%20related%20research%20for,you%20as%20an%20email%20alert)).

**Get a feed tuned to your taste** (learns from what you like over time)
- **[Scholar Inbox](https://www.scholar-inbox.com/landing)** — a daily digest that adapts to your ratings. Good default for a "morning coffee" feed.
- **[Research Rabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/)** — recommendations built from collections you assemble.
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/me/recommendations)** — recommendations from the papers in your library.
- **[Zeta Alpha](https://search.zeta-alpha.com/)** — neural search + recommendations aimed at AI/ML.
- **[PaperParrot](https://www.paperparrot.ai/)** — personalized new-paper alerts.

<details>
<summary>Tips</summary>

- Taste-based feeds are cold at first — rate a handful of papers up front and they improve quickly.
- Pick **one** feed tool and stick with it; running several just duplicates your inbox.
</details>

## Cover a whole field

*Best when you can't afford to miss anything in your area — comprehensive, at the cost of some noise or delay.*

- **Main conference proceedings** — the highest-quality filter, though it arrives months after the work appears.
- **[arXiv subscriptions](https://arxiv.org/help/subscribe)** — get every new submission in chosen categories by email. Comprehensive but high-volume.

<details>
<summary>Tips</summary>

- arXiv-by-category is a firehose — pair it with a trending or taste feed so you actually read the important ones.
</details>

## Keep track of what you've read

*Best for never losing a paper, and for citing it later without re-hunting.*

**Reference managers** (store, tag, cite; browser + Word/LaTeX integration)
- **Zotero** — free and open-source; the common recommendation for students.
- **Mendeley** — reference manager with a built-in PDF reader and social features.
- **EndNote** — established, often provided through university licenses.

**Lighter-weight options**
- **[Semantic Scholar Folders](https://www.semanticscholar.org/me/library/all)** — save papers and get notified when similar new ones appear.
- **Shared Google Doc or GitHub list** — public, editable, and easy to share, though not a convenient long-term library.
- **General note apps**
  - **Obsidian** — markdown notes linked to papers, extensible with plugins (and AI tooling such as Claude Code).
  - **Notion** — flexible database-style tracking of a reading list.

<details>
<summary>Tips</summary>

- Whatever you pick, install its **browser connector** — one-click saving is what makes you actually keep it up.
- A reference manager pays off most at writing time: it generates your bibliography for you.
</details>

## Find related papers

*Have a paper (or a question) and want its neighbors? Pick by how you want to explore.*

**Follow the citation graph** (visual — "what's near this paper")
- **[ConnectedPapers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/)** — one seed paper → a similarity graph of its neighborhood. Fast way to map an area.
- **[litmaps](https://app.litmaps.com/)** — like ConnectedPapers, but you build and track a living map over time.
- **[Research Rabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/)** — graph exploration plus ongoing recommendations.

**Ask a research question** (AI search over the literature)
- **[Asta / AI2 paper finder](https://paperfinder.allen.ai/)** — natural-language search tuned for scientific papers.
- **[Google AI-based search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_labs/search?hl=en)** — experimental natural-language search over Scholar.
- **[Elicit](https://elicit.com/)** — ask a question, get papers with extracted findings in a table. Best for evidence-gathering.
- **[Sourcely](https://www.sourcely.net/)** — AI paper search with summaries.

**Search classically** (keyword / author / who-cited-whom)
- **[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/)** — broadest coverage; use "cited by" for forward citations.
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org)** — cleaner metadata, citation context, and an influential-citation signal.
- **[Citation Gecko](https://citationgecko.azurewebsites.net/)** — start from seed papers and snowball through their citations.

**Low-tech but effective**
- **Read the related-work / background / intro** of a good recent paper — often the fastest curated map of an area.

<details>
<summary>Tips & caveats</summary>

- ConnectedPapers: use an incognito window if you go past the free-use limit; paid plans for heavy use.
- Citation-graph tools are great for *breadth*; a single well-written related-work section is great for *judgement about what matters*.
- ([Elicit was formerly at elicit.org](https://elicit.org/) — the current site is [elicit.com](https://elicit.com/).)
</details>
